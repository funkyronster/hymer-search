The gas system on classic Hymers generally works as follows.
There is an external gas locker towards the front - close by the passenger seat (LHD). Inside is space for 2 11kg gas bottles. But 11kg is a continental size. See specific post below for more info on this.
Mounted on the gas bottle in use, or sometimes mounted on the wall of the gas locker is a regulator. In pre 95 classic vans this should be 50mb, not 37mb as found in modern vans. See article below.
From the regulator a flexible pipe runs to a metal pipe, which then runs under or through the van to the gas distribution manifold. This is usually in a cupboard, usually not far from the kitchen. This has 4 or 5 red handled gas valves, with symbols on them - usually for cooker, fridge, heating and fridge. These valves are not intended for daily use - they are for isolating individual appliances for work and maintenance.
Each valve has a pipe that leads to the individual appliance.
If you have all sorts of extra stuff in your gas locker that looks more complicated than just a simple regulator, then you probably have optional extras that were popular in Germany in the 1990s. Usually these are to do with remote on/off switching and automatic bottle changeover - usually made by Truma. These don't age very well, and are often disconnected, but not removed completely. If in doubt, ask on the group and post photos.
Many vans have been converted to refillable bottles, or even an underslung gas tank. If you have these and are unsure - ask on the group. Scroll down for an article on underslung LPG tanks.
GENERAL GAS NOTES
Regulators have a 5 year lifespan. They usually have a date stamp. Same for flexible hoses in the gas locker.
There is no easy way to check how much gas you have left. The standard way is to run one bottle dry, then switch to the other full one. You soon get an idea how long a bottle lasts. You will use much more gas in winter if the heating is on constantly, than in summer, when you only use gas for cooking and fridge. A full 11 or 13kg bottle will last 1 or even 2 months in summer, but only 1 or 2 weeks in winter.
The gas distribution pipes around the van are steel. Copper pipes may also be used. There are different methods and connectors for steel and copper, so make sure you familiarise yourself with these.
This group (me!) believes that we all have a right to repair and maintain our own equipment, but it goes without saying that gas can be dangerous. However gas isn't that difficult to work on - the golden rule is to test every connection you work on with soapy water bubble detection. Twice! There are hundreds of Youtubes to guide you, plus this group of course.

TRUMA HEATERS - 3002/5002/2800 PLUS "TRUMAVENT"

OVERVIEW - TRUMA 3002/5002
Most pre 95 Hymers have a Truma 3002 heater. 6 series vans have a Truma 5002, which is bigger, having a double burner, but otherwise the same.
The way these fires work is by burning gas inside an enclosed aluminium box that has ribs on the outside. The fire radiates like a traditional fire, but also has a fan around the back which draws air from inside the van through the front of the fire and into hot air ducts that run around the van and have outlets at various points. Basically it is a blown air central heating system.
The fire is lit by a piezo electric igniter that makes a ticking sound. Power for the igniter is either provided by a small battery box that contains AA batteries, or by direct connection to the vans 12v supply.
To light the fire you push and turn the main (and only) control knob, the igniter will tick, the pilot/small burner should ignite, and then after a few seconds you release the knob and you get the familiar "whoosh" sound of the main burner igniting (if the knob setting is high enough).
The main knob is graduated 1 to 10, and the temperature of the fire is controlled by a thermostat which looks like a small copper tube cigar shape at the bottom of the fire, behind the main cover. The heat output is not continuously variable - the fire switches between the small burner and the big burner, regulated by the thermostat.
The fan is controlled by a small Truma control box, usually marked "Truma Vent", which contains a switch and a rotary dial. The switch is centre "off", up for manual, and down for automatic control of the fan speed. In the A position it is supposed to vary the speed of the fan (but not the fire setting) according to temperature. In the M position the fan speed is constant according to the dial position.
Truma stuff is remarkably reliable, but by now is well over 20 years old, so faults do eventually occur. Although they look complicated, they can be worked on and fixed, and spares are still available, but as with all things gas, it's at your own risk. If in doubt, seek out a qualified professional.
Earlier vans may have SL3/5002 heaters as opposed to the later S3/5002. Spares are hard to find for the SL models.
Ducting - the ducting system that runs around the van is essential to comfortable heating, but is prone to damage over the years. Many owners don't even know what it does! All owners should be aware of the heating ducts, and inspect and repair as needed. the outlets are round and at floor level. Each outlet has a butterfly flap to control output. With the fan on full, you should feel the air on the back of your hand from every outlet. if not, then there is a break somewhere. Repairs can be made with duct tape, and spares and ducting are standard and readily available.
The fan on the back of the fire also gets clogged with years of dust - there is a separate article about this.
COMMON FAULTS
Ignition - The AA batteries are flat.
Ticking but no ignition - The wire from the ignition box the igniter electrode is probably corroded away or worn, and the pulse of electricity to the igniter is grounding to chassis rather than getting to the electrode. Remedy - take the fire out and replace the HT wire - they are available. Or sometimes you can repair it with ptfe tape. Another trick is to try and slide an extra insulating tube along the wire to re-insulate a worn leaking bit.
No ticking even with new batteries - Possible wiring fault between the switch at the bottom of the main control rod and the igniter unit.
Popping sounds and minor explosions - Problems with the burners. The burners do not last forever. A common problem is that the thin metal gauze on the top of the burner - also called the burner crown - gets corroded or loose. This causes an uneven burn and all sorts of strange noises. Remedy - new burners, or if you are skilful, you can reattach the mesh using small screws or even brazing. This is quite a big job, but doable by a confident DIYer with a good toolbox, and some gas experience.
Fire won't light, or stay lit - This is usually a problem with the main valve that is at the bottom of the long control rod that leads down from the main knob. The main valve gets bunged up with fluff and dirt, and a good clean can help, along with vigorous working up and down of the rod. Quite often a sticking valve can mean that only the pilot light lights and the fire gets warm, but not hot, because the main burner isn't getting any gas.
Not much air coming out of the vents - clogged up fan - see blog link below.
Don't play with gas unless you are confident. I believe that owners have a right to maintain their own equipment, but along with that right comes the responsibility to yourself and your family.

TRUMA 2800 (as found in S700 and B694)
The big Hymers S700/B694 have an additional heater in the front, as well as a 3002 in the back which feeds the ducts. This is a Truma 2800 which sits under the front table, and just has 2 hot air outlets.
The Truma 2800 is a completely different beast to the 3002 - it doesn't radiate, it is solely a hot air fan heater. Unlike the 3002, this heater can be used while driving.
The control box is usually marked "Trumatic" with 2 switches and a dial. The left switch is up for low power, down for high power. The right switch is centre "off", down for fan only (no heat), up for heat. The dial is a thermostat, and will switch the fire on or off according to ambient temperature. There is no continuous fan speed control - the fan is either low or high speed according to the left switch position.
When you switch it on, the fan starts, and about 20 seconds later you should hear a loud click, followed by a whoosh as the burner ignites. There is a red LED on the control box which only lights when there is a fault.
The commonest fault is when there is no click, and the light comes on. This is caused by either no gas - ie your bottles/tank is empty, or by a faulty gas control solenoid valve, or by low voltage.
The 2800 will not work unless the voltage is above a certain level - about 12v. If you hear no click, the first thing to do is to check the voltage at the fire, and compare it with the voltage at the battery. Quite often, the terminals supplying battery voltage to the fire have become corroded, resulting in a voltage drop, which prevents the fire from starting. Cleaning these terminals can often cure this fault. A useful tip if you have this problem is to start the engine, which will boost the voltage. If the fire starts with the engine running, but won't start with the engine off, then that is a sure sign of voltage drop, and that the fire is otherwise OK.
Unfortunately, spares for the 2800 are becoming difficult to find. There is a company in Germany that specialises in Truma circuit board repair and spares. www.trumareparatur.de But before you assume that the circuit board is faulty there are a couple of checks you must do - too much to write here, but ask the question if you are in this situation.
If the fire works OK in either low or high power mode, but not in the other, then the circuit board will be OK but one of the solenoid valves is sticking or faulty. This can be repaired by taking the fire out and opening the case, but solenoid valves are difficult to obtain.

GAS COOKER - HOB - OVENS - OVERVIEW
Several different gas hobs have been used in classic Hymers - the two main manufacturers are Smev and Cramer - both now owned by Dometic.
Hobs generally last maintenance free for decades. The two main problems are dirt and corrosion.
The flame fingers should be pointy and blue. Orange is usually an indication of contamination - usually water from cleaning or old food.
The burner consists of a round metal thing - called a burner crown - this is a useful search term when looking for spares and info.
OVENS. Ovens were never a factory option on pre 95 Hymers. If you have an oven - usually a Smev - then it has been installed as an extra either by a previous owner or a dealer, usually at the expense of a cupboard!
Microwaves are the devil's work - you are on your own!

HABITATION OVERVIEW
Habitation is the generic trade word for the living side of the van. Basically this is everything inside the van behind the driver and passenger. It includes all the furniture, woodwork, non vehicle electrics, gas appliances, water system - you get the picture.
But to keep the indexes manageable, there is a separate index for GAS, FRIDGES and ELECTRICS, so this index is for everything else habitation.
There is also a BODYWORK index which is for all things outside the van.
WINDOWS BLINDS ROOFLIGHTS and STEPS are classed as habitation, so are in this index.
Make sure you expand all the posts to see all the info, whenever you see "view more" or "replies"
The index is organised by subject, with all the relevant posts below each subject.
OVERVIEW
In general everything inside the van is removable - I have seen some owners strip and rebuild a van inside completely.
The cabinet (wood) work is held together with fittings similar in concept, but not identical to fitted kitchen cabinets. Classic Hymers generally are free of expensive custom plastic panels and mouldings found in modern vans, although there are some in the bathroom.
The floor is a sandwich of plywood with an insulating core and faced with a linoleum style finish. Carpets are usually fitted, but are not necessary if you prefer, but most vans are on their second or third carpets by now. If re-carpeting, it makes sense to go for removable carpets if possible.
The drop down bed has gas struts to help lifting and lowering - these struts generally wear out after 10 or 20 years but are replaceable - info below. The drop down bed mattress is an odd shape but there are plenty of custom mattress makers and foam cutters in every big town. Rear beds in the bigger vans are foam mattresses on wooden slats or metal frames with springs. Mattresses and cushions get tired with age, and are easy to replace.
The bathroom has a sink and shower tray - these are expensive to replace and should be treated with care, especially shower trays. But the parts are available, or can be repaired. Mirrors are 3mm mirror glass. 3mm mirror glass is not a stock size and is very hard to get. But 3mm acrylic (plastic) mirror is readily available, and is generally used to replace broken mirrors.
The toilet is usually a Thetford C3 bench toilet, very popular, spares easily available. You can buy a "Fresh up Kit" from Thetford suppliers which comprises a new cassette and toilet seat - always a good idea on a new secondhand van!
The kitchen is usually a unit with hob built in, cupboards and fridge below. There is a basic extractor fan.
Most vans have a "furry" lining to the roof. This can be cleaned with effort! See below.
Windows and doors have their own overview below.

OVERVIEW
The brown tinted plastic windows are made by Seitz (now Dometic) and are usually from their S3 range. Most windows open outwards, but some vans have sliding windows.
Seitz S3 windows work on a modular cassette system comprising of an inner, which contains the inner frame, blind and flyscreen, and an outer, which is sealed to the wall externally, screws go through the inner into the outer and clamp the two to the wall in a sandwich. All windows need resealing at some point, and this is a popular topic in the indexes. Never use silicone - silicone is obsolete!
Everything to do with windows can be fixed or refurbished, and if the glass is broken, there are other suppliers in addition to Dometic Seitz.
The glass windows in the cab generally give no trouble. If broken it's usually an insurance job and spares and replacements are available.

BATHROOM - NOT INCLUDING TOILET
Most spares are still available. Common problems are leaking or not working taps, easily fixed. Familiarise yourself with where the water pipes connect to the taps, and feel for leaks regularly - at least once a month. Nothing is worse than an undetected drip drip leak - it can cause substantial rot damage if unattended.
If your van has a concertina shower cabinet door with plastic panels, treat it with care - it is one of the very few things we have never located original spares or replacements for. If you smash it, you are faced with fitting a new one from another manufacturer, or replacing with a curtain.
Shower trays can get cracked and leak - this must be fixed to avoid rotting the floor. Most shower trays are either available from Hymer, or GRP Leisure. Shower tray replacement is a major job, usually involving a complete bathroom strip and rebuild, but it is doable.

TOILET
It's no fun working on a smelly Thetford, but nothing is more important than a comfortable working toilet. All the spares are available, and if you make the effort to work out how it works, you will be rewarded. Everything is fixable, and replacement is never necessary.
A classic newbie mistake is to leave a heavy toilet bag or other item on top of the big flush button, which then fills the bowl and overflows - don't be that person!
Common faults
No flush - When you press the flush, it operates a switch at the bottom of the flush button shaft, but there is no access from above. The switch is mounted in the roof of the cassette compartment - use a mirror or phone to inspect. From the switch the wires go to another switch that is operated by the tab on the cassette - a safety mechanism to prevent flush when cassette not present. All these wires have connections the can get damaged or corroded. All can be fixed - read the posts below fr detailed info and photos.
If all the wires are OK then the flush switch operates the relay that operates the 12v solenoid valve in the flush water feed. And also the main water pump. Both the relay and the valve can fail. Access is via removal of the toilet roll holder.
Water seeping into bowl through flush. This can be frustrating because it fills up the cassette too quickly. The common cause is dirt and grit in the solenoid valve preventing it from closing fully, so water can seep into the bowl. The fix is to clean or replace the valve. See below. The quick fix if you are on holiday is to disconnect the water pump, and release the pressure in the water pipes by opening a tap. This will then prevent water seepage into the bowl - this is useful to prevent seepage overnight. Not an idea or permanent fix, but a get out of seepage jail card. See below.

IMPORTANT NOTE - READ THIS BEFORE ORDERING GAS STRUTS.
It appears that the strut listed by SGS on their web page as suitable for Hymer beds is wrong. Several owners have ordered from SGS and it has been too short. The full extension dimension should be 380mm.
However the part listed by the Dutch group in their excellent article (linked below), available from Hinscha in Belgium is correct.
https://www.hinscha.com/.../gas-spring-stabilus-lift-o...
The part numbers from both companies are very similar - there seems to be confusion in the 5th character - it should be a letter i, and not a letter L. Lowercase L and Uppercase i look identical on screen, and I suspect this is the root of the problem.
So if ordering from SGS - be sure to refer them to the Hinscha part - 3571iy - and not the SGS part 3571LY.

WINDCREEN WIPERS
BACKGROUND
The main thing to remember about the wiper motor is that it is not a base chassis part - so it is no use trying to order a wiper motor based on your Merc or Fiat chassis model. The design and construction of the wiper mechanism and motor was down to Hymer, because the bodywork on which the wipers sit is part of the Hymer body, not the base chassis. Hymer used wiper components from different sources, and unfortunately there is no definitive list of what was used where. We have had to piece it together over the years. We now have compatible part numbers that have been found out by several owners - you should read the various posts below and see which one might fit your van. I believe, but can not guarantee, that in general, Hymer used similar motors right across the B and S ranges, up to 1995.
The wiper motor used in general was a Bosch style from the 80s and 90s, and was used in thousands of different vehicles. So generally it can be done - but the problem is getting something that will just easily screw on, and has the same connection plug. Unless you take off the dash board, access to the motor through the bonnet hatch is really difficult and can be extremely frustrating, but it can be done with patience.
